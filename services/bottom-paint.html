Bottom paint & antifouling

Growth on the bottom costs you speed, fuel and engine life. The right antifouling depends on your hull, how long the boat stays in the water and how warm and shallow your harbor is. Tell us about the boat, and a local yard will recommend and quote the paint.

Get a quote Call

What's included

  • Haul-out and pressure wash
  • Sanding or stripping old paint, with dust contained
  • Epoxy barrier coat on new or blistered hulls
  • Ablative or hard antifouling chosen for your use
  • Copper-free options for aluminum hulls and drives
  • Running gear, zinc and anode replacement

Signs you need it

  • Slime, weed or barnacles within weeks of launch
  • The boat is slower or burns more fuel than it used to
  • The paint is flaking or has worn through to the gelcoat
  • You're switching from trailer storage to a slip or mooring

On Cape Cod & the Islands

Warm, shallow bays like Waquoit, Popponesset, Pleasant Bay and Lewis Bay often grow faster than deeper, cooler water in Vineyard Sound or Buzzards Bay. A boat that sits on a mooring all summer needs different protection than one that's trailered home every weekend.

Questions to ask any shop

  • Ablative or hard paint for how I use the boat, and why?
  • Is the old paint compatible, or does it need stripping or a primer?
  • Is the paint safe for my aluminum outdrive or hull?
  • Will you replace the zincs or anodes at the same time?

FAQ

Bottom paint: common questions

Ablative or hard bottom paint?

Ablative paint wears away slowly as the boat moves, so it suits boats that run often and can be hauled and relaunched. Hard paint makes a tougher surface for boats that sit, or that get scrubbed or race. A local yard can recommend one based on your harbor and season.

Can I put copper paint on an aluminum boat?

Not cuprous-oxide (copper) paints directly on aluminum. They can cause galvanic corrosion. Aluminum hulls and outdrives need paints made for them. Tell us the hull material in your request.

Do trailered boats need bottom paint?

Often not, if the boat comes out of the water after every trip. If it stays in the water for days or weeks at a time, ask a yard.
