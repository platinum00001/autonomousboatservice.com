Spring commissioning & launch

Spring commissioning is everything between "the wrap comes off" and "we're running to the Vineyard." Memorial Day is the Cape's busiest deadline. Tell us when you want to be on the water and we'll find a shop with an opening.

Get a quote Call

What's included

  • Removing shrink-wrap and recycling it where available
  • Reinstalling and testing batteries, and checking the charging system
  • Engine start-up and check: cooling flow, belts, hoses, clamps, steering and trim
  • Checking through-hulls, seacocks, bilge pumps and float switches
  • Fresh-water system flush and refill
  • Bottom paint touch-up or a full coat before launch
  • A safety-gear check: life jackets, throwable, fire extinguisher, flares and horn
  • Launch, or delivery to your slip or mooring

Signs you need it

  • Your boat was winterized by a different shop than the one launching it
  • You want to launch before Memorial Day
  • Last season ended with a problem you never fixed
  • The boat sat for more than one winter

On Cape Cod & the Islands

On the Cape, the launch date also depends on your harbor: moorings get set, docks go in and launch services start on their own schedules. If you're on a town mooring, check that your permit and mooring inspection are current. Many towns require inspections on a regular cycle.

Questions to ask any shop

  • What's on your commissioning checklist, and is it a flat price or hourly?
  • Will you call me before doing extra work you find?
  • Do you deliver to my mooring or slip, or do I pick the boat up?
  • Can you change the impeller and gear oil at the same time?

FAQ

Spring commissioning: common questions

How early should I book spring commissioning?

Late winter. By April, most Cape yards are booked through Memorial Day. If you want to launch in May, request it in February or March.

Is commissioning the same as an annual engine service?

Not always. Commissioning gets the boat running and safe. An annual or hours-based service (oil, filters, plugs, impeller, gear oil) follows your engine maker's schedule. Ask to bundle them.

What safety gear do I need on board?

U.S. Coast Guard rules depend on boat size and type. Most boats need a wearable life jacket for each person, a throwable device on boats 16 feet and longer, a sound-producing device and visual distress signals for coastal waters. Many boats also need a fire extinguisher. Check the current Coast Guard requirements for your boat.
