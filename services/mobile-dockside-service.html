Mobile & dockside boat service

On the Cape, a lot of boats live on moorings or on trailers in the driveway. Mobile and dockside shops come to the boat for diagnostics, tune-ups, winterizing and many repairs. Tell us exactly where the boat is and how to reach it.

What's included

  • Dockside and mooring diagnostics
  • Tune-ups, impellers, filters and fluids
  • Driveway winterizing and shrink-wrap
  • Battery and electrical troubleshooting
  • Pre-trip checks and pre-purchase engine checks
  • Arranging a haul-out when the job needs one

Signs you need it

  • The boat runs but has an intermittent problem
  • You can't easily trailer or move the boat
  • You want service on a set day at your own dock
  • You're buying a used boat and want the engine looked at

On Cape Cod & the Islands

To reach a boat on a mooring, a mobile shop needs your harbor, the mooring number or position, and how to get out to it: a launch, your dinghy or the shop's own skiff. On busy summer harbors, early-morning appointments are usually easiest.

Questions to ask any shop

  • Is there a trip charge, and how is travel time billed?
  • Can you reach a boat on a mooring, or do I need to bring it to a dock?
  • Do you carry common parts on the truck?
  • When does a job become a haul-out job?

FAQ

Mobile & dockside service: common questions

Is mobile service more expensive?

Sometimes there's a trip charge, but you save the haul, the trailer run and the time. For many small and medium jobs it works out about even. Ask each shop how it bills.

What can't be done on a mooring?

Anything that needs the boat out of the water: bottom paint, running gear, through-hulls, most lower-unit and outdrive work, and big engine jobs. A mobile shop can often arrange the haul-out for you.

Do marinas allow outside mechanics?

Policies vary. Some marinas and yards only allow their own staff or approved contractors. Check with your marina, or tell us where you're kept and we'll take its policy into account.
