Signs you need it

  • Electronics reboot when you start the engine
  • Batteries die overnight
  • Green corrosion on terminals or behind the panel
  • Breakers trip, or a fuse keeps blowing

On Cape Cod & the Islands

Electrical problems on Cape boats are usually corrosion. Salt fog and condensation get into every connector, and most failures come down to a bad connection. Lithium batteries and newer electronics need the right charging and wiring, so ask a shop before upgrading.

Questions to ask any shop

  • Do you follow ABYC electrical standards?
  • Are you an authorized installer for my brand of electronics?
  • Will you label the wiring and give me a diagram?
  • Can you test the boat for stray current or bonding problems?

FAQ

Electrical & electronics: common questions

Can I install a chartplotter myself?

Simple installs, yes. Networked systems, radar, autopilots and anything that ties into engine data are where a pro saves time and headaches, and some brands require an authorized installer to keep the warranty.

Should I switch to lithium batteries?

They're lighter and last longer, but they need a compatible charger, an alternator setup that can handle them, and correct wiring. Ask a shop to check your system before you buy.

What's an MMSI number and do I need one?

It's the ID that lets a DSC VHF radio send a distress call with your position at the press of a button. Recreational boaters can get one from BoatUS and other authorized issuers. It's worth having.
