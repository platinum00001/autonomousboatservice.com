Choosing bottom paint for Cape Cod and Islands waters

How to pick antifouling bottom paint on Cape Cod, the Vineyard and Nantucket: ablative or hard, copper or copper-free, aluminum hulls and local growing conditions.

There's no single best bottom paint. The right one depends on how warm and shallow your harbor is, how long the boat stays in the water, how often you run it, and what the hull and drive are made of.

1. How you use the boat

  • Trailered and hauled after every trip: you may not need antifouling at all. Rinse and dry the boat after each trip.
  • On a mooring or slip all season, used often: ablative (self-polishing) paints wear slowly as the boat moves through the water, exposing fresh biocide. It's a popular choice on the Cape.
  • Sits for long stretches, or gets scrubbed or raced: hard (modified epoxy) paints leave a tougher, scrubbable surface, but they build up layers over the years.

2. Your water

Warm, shallow, protected bays such as Waquoit, Popponesset, Pleasant Bay, Lewis Bay and the salt ponds often grow slime, weed and barnacles faster in July and August than deeper, cooler, current-swept water in Vineyard Sound, Buzzards Bay or off the Canal. Boats on warm-water moorings usually need stronger protection, or a mid-season haul and scrub.

3. Hull and drive material

  • Fiberglass: most paints work. New or blistered hulls benefit from an epoxy barrier coat first.
  • Aluminum hulls, outdrives and outboard lower units: don't use cuprous-oxide (copper) paint directly on aluminum. It can cause galvanic corrosion. Use a paint labeled for aluminum, usually copper-free.
  • Wood: ask a yard with wooden-boat experience. Some traditional boats use different systems.

4. Compatibility

Different paint types don't always stick to each other. Putting ablative over hard paint, or a new chemistry over old layers, may need a sealer, a primer or stripping. Know what's on your bottom now. Your yard's records or an old can are the easiest way to find out.

5. The environment

Antifouling works by being toxic to growth, so it matters how it's applied and removed. Professional yards contain sanding dust and wash water. Copper-free and lower-copper options keep improving. Ask your yard about them, especially if you're near shellfish beds or a sensitive pond.

Quick answers

  • Most Cape mooring boats: an ablative paint suited to the region, applied over the right primer.
  • Aluminum anything: an aluminum-safe paint, copper-free.
  • Racing or frequently scrubbed: a hard paint, burnished.

Common questions

How often does bottom paint need redoing on Cape Cod?

Many mooring boats are recoated every season or two, depending on the paint and how fast the boat grows. Your yard can tell you from the condition at haul-out.

Can I launch right after painting?

Every paint has a minimum dry time, and many have a maximum time before launch. Follow the can and your yard's schedule.
