Spring commissioning checklist for Cape Cod boaters

A printable spring commissioning checklist for Cape Cod and Islands boats: hull, engine, electrical, plumbing, safety gear, paperwork and launch day.

On Cape Cod, the season starts in a rush. Yards launch boats in a set order, harbors set moorings, and Memorial Day comes fast. Work through this list, or hand it to your shop, and your first trip won't end in a tow.

Paperwork and permits

  • Massachusetts registration or federal documentation is current, and the decals are on
  • Town mooring or slip permit renewed, and any required mooring inspection done
  • Insurance active, with the cruising area covering where you'll go (the Islands, for example)
  • Towing membership renewed, if you have one

Hull and deck

  • Shrink-wrap off and recycled where a program exists
  • Hull checked for cracks, blisters and impact damage from winter storage
  • Bottom paint touched up or redone. Most antifouling has a minimum and maximum time before launch, so check the can.
  • Zincs and anodes replaced if they're more than half worn
  • Drain plug in. Write it on your launch checklist.
  • Cleats, rails and canvas checked for loose fasteners

Engine and drive

  • Water-pump impeller checked or replaced on your maker's schedule
  • Gear oil checked for water, and engine oil and filters done if not done in the fall
  • Fuel lines, primer bulb and clamps checked for cracking
  • Fuel-water separator filter replaced
  • Belts, hoses and clamps checked (inboards and sterndrives), and sterndrive bellows checked
  • Steering and trim worked lock to lock, and fluid levels checked
  • First start on muffs or in the water: strong tell-tale stream, no alarms

Electrical

  • Batteries charged, load-tested and reinstalled with clean, tight terminals
  • Navigation lights, horn, bilge pumps and float switches tested
  • Electronics updated, and the VHF radio checked (with DSC and your MMSI entered)

Plumbing

  • Through-hulls and seacocks opened and closed, and hose clamps checked (double-clamp below the waterline)
  • Fresh-water system flushed of antifreeze
  • Head and holding tank checked

Safety gear

  • Wearable life jackets for everyone, in sizes that fit, plus a throwable on boats 16 feet and longer
  • Visual distress signals in date, plus a sound-producing device
  • Fire extinguisher, if your boat requires one, checked and in date
  • Anchor, chain and rode suited to the depths where you'll anchor
  • First-aid kit, a flashlight and a paper chart of your area

Launch day

  • Check the drain plug again
  • Run the blower before starting gas inboards
  • Watch the bilge for the first hour in the water
  • Short shakedown run before the first long trip

Requirements change, so check the current U.S. Coast Guard equipment rules for your boat's size and type.

Common questions

When should I book commissioning on Cape Cod?

In February or March if you want to launch in May. Calendars fill up before Memorial Day.

How often should I replace the water-pump impeller?

Follow your engine maker's schedule. Many suggest every few seasons or a set number of hours. Running in sandy, shallow water wears them out sooner.
