Outboard won't start? Check these first

Twelve quick, safe checks when your outboard won't start or dies: kill switch, fuel, primer bulb, vent, battery, neutral and more, plus when to call a mechanic.

Many no-starts are something simple. Work through these in order. They're quick, and most need no tools. If you're on the water and drifting toward danger, anchor first. If anyone is at risk, call the Coast Guard on VHF 16.

  1. Kill-switch lanyard. Is the clip on the switch? It's the most common no-start there is.
  2. In neutral. Most engines won't crank in gear. Wiggle the shifter in neutral.
  3. Tank vent open. A closed vent on a portable tank starves the engine after a few minutes of running.
  4. Fuel in the tank, and the right tank selected. Fuel gauges lie. Look or dip the tank.
  5. Primer bulb. Squeeze until it's firm. If it never firms up, the fitting or bulb may be leaking or the line may be kinked.
  6. Fuel line connections. Check that quick-connects click home at both ends.
  7. Battery switch and battery. Is the switch on, and are the terminals tight and clean? Slow cranking usually means a weak battery or a bad connection.
  8. Fuses and breakers. A blown fuse can kill the starter or ignition.
  9. Choke or prime (older engines). Follow the cold-start procedure for your engine.
  10. Flooded? If you smell fuel, wait a few minutes and try again with the throttle slightly open, if your manual allows that.
  11. Water in the fuel. Look at the fuel-water separator bowl. Water or debris there points to fuel trouble.
  12. Alarms. Note any beeps or warning lights. They're clues for the mechanic.

Stop and call a mechanic if…

  • You hear grinding, knocking or a clunk
  • There's no tell-tale water stream after starting (shut it down)
  • The overheat alarm sounds
  • Fuel is leaking anywhere
  • It starts and then dies again and again

Write down what happened, the engine make and model, and the hours, and take a short video if you can. Then send an engine repair request. Mobile shops can often check it at your slip or mooring.

Common questions

Why does my outboard start and then die?

Common causes are a closed tank vent, a fuel-line air leak, a clogged filter, water in the fuel or an idle problem. If the simple checks don't fix it, have a mechanic check the fuel system.

Is it safe to run an outboard on muffs?

Yes, with full water flow before you start. Keep clear of the prop, and don't rev the engine. Shut it down at once if the tell-tale stream stops.
